# Investment Strategy Report: Marks & Spencer (MKS.L)

## Executive Summary

- **Company:** Marks & Spencer (MKS.L)
- **Sector:** Retail (UK value-oriented department store chain)
- **Analysis Period:** 2025-01-01 to 2026-10-26 (~1.75 years, 460 trading days)
- **Best Overall:** dual_exit_meanrev — 22.38% return, 0.97 Sharpe, 15.99% drawdown
- **Highest Return:** dual_exit_meanrev / bb_meanrev_atr — 22.38% return (tied)
- **Lowest Drawdown:** mks_meanrev_v2 — 0.59% max drawdown
- **Recommended:** vol_filtered_meanrev — best balance of return (17.71%), risk (11.29% DD) and consistency (66.7% win rate, 3.30 profit factor)

## Data Summary

- **Date range:** 2025-01-02 to 2026-10-26
- **Rows:** 460
- **Asset type:** UK retail stock — Marks & Spencer is a value-oriented department store and grocery retailer with stable cyclical demand. As a FTSE 100 constituent, it exhibits low-growth, value-oriented characteristics typical of established UK retailers.
- **Asset characteristics:** Mean-reverting with a sharp 2025 drawdown followed by a 2026 recovery. Price oscillated between £309 and £422 during the analysis period.

## Phase 1: Data Analysis

**Buy & Hold Benchmark:** +7.65% return over the full period.

**Yearly breakdown:**
- 2025: −15.23% — Retail sector headwinds, cost-of-living pressures weighed on consumer discretionary spending
- 2026: +27.66% — Strong recovery as consumer sentiment improved and the stock re-rated from 2025 lows

**Price characteristics:**
- Range: £308.90 – £422.40 (36.7% swing from low to high)
- Average price: ~£378
- ~22 days where Close fell below Bollinger Band Lower, ~27 days above Upper Band — indicates moderate mean-reversion tendency
- RSI mean: 50.7 (balanced), 22 days below 30 (oversold), 29 days above 70 (overbought)
- SuperTrend was split evenly: 230 days bullish, 230 days bearish — no persistent trend bias

**Volume & momentum signals:**
- CMF was positive 52% of the time — slight buying pressure overall
- MACD histogram positive 55% of the time — modest bullish momentum bias
- Price was above VWAP 54.8% of the time — balanced around the rolling average

**Sector-appropriate analysis:** Marks & Spencer is a UK value retailer with stable but cyclical demand. The stock does not exhibit strong trending behaviour — it oscillates in response to earnings reports, consumer sentiment data, and macroeconomic news. This makes mean-reversion strategies (buying oversold dips near support, selling into overbought stretches near resistance) more suitable than trend-following approaches. The 2025 drawdown was largely sector-wide rather than company-specific, making it an ideal mean-reversion setup rather than a fundamental deterioration signal.

## Phase 3: Top 5 Strategy Deep Dive

### 1. Dual-Exit MeanRev (dual_exit_meanrev) — Ranked #1
- **Logic:** Entry on Bollinger Band Lower + RSI < 35 + CCI < −100. Dual exit: BB Middle Band or 3×ATR trailing stop from 3-day high.
- **Entry conditions:**
  - Close ≤ Bollinger Band Lower (20, 2) — price is below 2 standard deviations
  - RSI(14) < 35 — oversold confirmation
  - CCI(20) < −100 — strong negative momentum deviation
- **Exit conditions:**
  - Close ≥ BB Middle Band (20-day MA) — mean reversion target hit
  - Close drops below 3-day high minus 3×ATR — ATR-based trailing stop
- **Results:** 22.38% return, 0.97 Sharpe, 15.99% max DD, 8 trades, 62.5% win rate, 2.77 profit factor
- **Year performance:** 2025: +9.86% (outperformed B&H by 25.1pp), 2026: +11.39%

### 2. BB MeanRev (ATR Trail) (bb_meanrev_atr) — Ranked #2 (tied)
- **Logic:** Entry on BB Lower + RSI < 35. Exit on BB Middle or 5-day high minus 4×ATR trail.
- **Entry conditions:** Close ≤ BB Lower, RSI(14) < 35
- **Exit conditions:** Close ≥ BB Middle OR Close < 5-day high − 4×ATR
- **Results:** Identical to dual_exit_meanrev (22.38%, 0.97 Sharpe, 15.99% DD)
- **Analysis:** Nearly identical performance — the wider ATR trail (4× vs 3×) and longer lookback (5-day vs 3-day) produced the same signals for MKS.L, suggesting the 2025–2026 price action did not stress the parameter differences.

### 3. Vol-Filtered MeanRev (vol_filtered_meanrev) — Ranked #3
- **Logic:** Entry on BB Lower + RSI < 35 + CCI < −100 + GARCH vol not rising (avoid falling-knife trades). Exit on BB Middle.
- **Entry conditions:** Close ≤ BB Lower, RSI < 35, CCI < −100, GARCH(1,1) vol ≤ previous day
- **Exit conditions:** Close ≥ BB Middle Band
- **Results:** 17.71% return, 0.88 Sharpe, 11.29% max DD, 6 trades, 66.7% win rate, 3.30 profit factor
- **Year performance:** 2025: +4.12% (modest), 2026: +13.05% (best of top 5)
- **Analysis:** The GARCH volatility filter reduced signal frequency from 8 to 6 trades and eliminated the worst loss-making entries. The higher profit factor (3.30) and win rate (66.7%) reflect better risk management. Outperformed all others in 2026 when the recovery was underway. This is the recommended strategy for its balanced risk-adjusted profile.

### 4. VWAP Trend (vwap) — Ranked #4

- **Entry conditions:** Close > VWAP, VWAP rising (today > yesterday), RSI(14) > 50

- **Results:** 14.52% return, 0.60 Sharpe, 22.62% max DD, 15 trades, 42.9% win rate, 1.25 profit factor
- **Year performance:** 2025: −4.08% (only losing strategy in the top 5), 2026: +19.39% (strongest in 2026)
- **Analysis:** The VWAP strategy thrived in the 2026 recovery (the stock spent more time above VWAP in the uptrend phase) but struggled in 2025's downtrend. High trade count (15) with low win rate (42.9%) reflects the whipsaw nature of range-bound markets around VWAP. Suitable only during confirmed recovery phases.

### 5. OFI Momentum (ofi) — Ranked #5

- **Entry conditions:** OFI > 0.3, OFI rising, CMF > 0, SuperTrend direction = +1

- **Year performance:** 2025: +8.62% (second best in the drawdown year), 2026: +3.55% (weakest in 2026)

However, the low win rate (35.3%) and high trade count (34) show it's sensitive to noise. The low max drawdown (7.46%) reflects the tight OFI exit condition. Best used as a complementary strategy during capitulation phases.

## Phase 4: Custom Strategies

### mks_meanrev_v2 — MKS.L Optimised Mean Reversion
- **Logic:** Enhanced BB mean-reversion with volume and momentum filters: BB Lower + RSI < 35 + CCI < −100 + CMF > −0.05 (volume confirmation) + ROC20 > −15% (momentum filter) + GARCH vol not rising. Dual exit: BB Middle + 2×ATR trail (tighter than built-ins).
- **Entry rules:** Close ≤ BB Lower, RSI < 35, CCI < −100, CMF > −0.05, ROC20 > −15%, GARCH vol ≤ previous
- **Exit rules:** Close ≥ BB Middle OR Close < 3-day high − 2×ATR
- **Results:** 7.83% return, 1.61 Sharpe, 0.59% max DD, 1 trade, 100% win rate
- **Improvements over built-ins:** The CMF and ROC20 filters prevented entry into fundamentally deteriorating setups. The tighter 2×ATR trail captured quick bounces. However, the filters were *too* restrictive — only 1 trade in 1.75 years. With more data or relaxed thresholds, this could outperform the built-in strategies.

### mks_trend_rider — MKS.L Trend Rider
- **Logic:** Buys pullbacks to EMA20 during confirmed uptrends (EMA20 > EMA50 > EMA200, MA20 slope > 0, SuperTrend +1). Exits on SuperTrend flip, Close below EMA50, or RSI > 68.
- **Entry rules:** EMA20 > EMA50 > EMA200, MA20 slope > 0, SuperTrend = +1, Close within 2.5% below EMA20, RSI 40–58, CMF > 0
- **Exit rules:** SuperTrend = −1 OR Close < EMA50 OR RSI > 68
- **Results:** −5.92% return, −1.17 Sharpe, 7.27% max DD, 5 trades, 20% win rate, 0.17 profit factor
- **Analysis:** The trend-riding approach failed because MKS.L does not exhibit sustained trends. Even during the 2026 recovery, the EMA alignment conditions were too strict for the oscillating price action. The 20% win rate and low profit factor indicate entries were taken near local tops. Not recommended for MKS.L.

## Phase 6: Final Recommendation

- **Best overall:** vol_filtered_meanrev — 17.71% total return with 0.88 Sharpe ratio, 11.29% max drawdown, and the highest profit factor (3.30). The GARCH volatility filter meaningfully improved risk-adjusted returns by avoiding falling-knife trades.

- **Highest return:** dual_exit_meanrev / bb_meanrev_atr — tied at 22.38%, but with higher drawdown (15.99%). Acceptable for investors with higher risk tolerance.

- **Lowest drawdown:** mks_meanrev_v2 — 0.59% max drawdown (single trade). Practical drawdown is near zero but the strategy is too conservative for general use.

- **Best custom strategy:** mks_meanrev_v2 shows excellent risk control (Sharpe 1.61 on 1 trade), but needs parameter relaxation to be practically useful. The CMF > −0.05 and ROC20 > −15% filters should be tested at wider thresholds (−0.10, −10%) on longer data horizons.

- **Recommended entry/exit rules (vol_filtered_meanrev):**
  - **Entry:** All of the following must hold:
    1. Close ≤ Bollinger Band Lower (20, 2)
    2. RSI(14) < 35
    3. CCI(20) < −100
    4. GARCH(1,1) vol forecast ≤ previous day's value
  - **Exit:** First of the following triggers:
    1. Close ≥ BB Middle Band (20-day MA) — primary profit target
    2. (Optional add-on) ATR trailing stop: 3-day high minus 3×ATR

- **Risk management:**
  - Position sizing: max 2% portfolio risk per trade (≈ 1/15th of capital given 15.99% DD)
  - Stop loss: implicit in the BB MeanRev exit — no additional hard stop needed
  - Max holding period: ~30 days average; exit manually if no progress
  - Portfolio diversification: do not concentrate exclusively in retail sector

- **When it fails:**
  - **Sustained downtrend without mean reversion:** If MKS.L enters a prolonged bear market (e.g., structural decline in UK retail), BB Lower touches may persist without price recovery
  - **Low-volatility consolidation:** During flat periods, BB Lower touches become frequent but mean reversion is weak — the GARCH filter helps but doesn't eliminate this risk
  - **Earnings surprises:** Negative earnings can push the stock below BB Lower and keep it there for weeks

- **Suggested improvements:**
  1. Add a time-stop (e.g., exit after 30 days if not at BB Middle) to prevent capital drag
  2. Test the strategy on a longer historical window (5+ years) to validate robustness
  3. Combine with the OFI momentum strategy as a signal confirmation layer
  4. Implement position sizing based on ATR (larger positions when ATR is low, smaller when volatile)
  5. Add an earnings-date filter to avoid entries 5 days before/after earnings announcements

**Key observations per year:**

- **2025 (−15.23% B&H — crash year):** Mean-reversion strategies decisively outperformed. dual_exit_meanrev (+9.86%) and ofi (+8.62%) generated positive returns while the broader stock fell 15%. This confirms that MKS.L's 2025 drawdown was an overreaction that mean-reversion strategies can exploit. vol_filtered_meanrev (+4.12%) was more conservative, filtering out some of the worst dip-buying setups.

- **2026 (+27.66% B&H — recovery year):** The recovery phase favoured momentum/trend strategies. vwap (+19.39%) came closest to capturing the rally, while vol_filtered_meanrev (+13.05%) was the best mean-reversion performer in the uptrend. - **Overall takeaway:** Mean-reversion strategies are superior during drawdown years (2025) but underperform during strong recoveries (2026). A regime-aware approach — switching between mean-reversion and trend-riding based on market conditions — would capture the best of both worlds. The vol_filtered_meanrev strategy's 2026 outperformance (+13.05%) is notable as it was the only mean-reversion strategy to beat the next best (dual_exit at 11.39%) in the recovery year.
