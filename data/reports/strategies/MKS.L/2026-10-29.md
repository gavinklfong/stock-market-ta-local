Marks & Spencer is a UK value-oriented retailer with stable cyclical demand. The stock had a massive recovery in 2023 (+115%) and 2024 (+36%), a ~15% drawdown in 2025, and a ~28% recovery in 2026. The stock exhibits strong mean-reverting behavior around its 20-day moving average with periodic overshoots below the Bollinger Band lower band — making mean-reversion strategies the clear choice.

### Analysis

**dual_exit_meanrev** and **bb_meanrev_atr** produce identical results (+22.38%, Sharpe 0.97, 8 trades) — the different exit parameters (3-day vs 5-day high, 3×ATR vs 4×ATR) have no practical effect because the BB Middle exit triggers first in all 8 trades.

**vol_filtered_meanrev** is the best risk-adjusted built-in (Sharpe 0.88, Calmar 1.22, PF 3.30) — the GARCH filter reduces signals from 14 to 7 and improves quality. But lower total return (+17.71%) than dual_exit.

**ema_pullback_vol** requires EMA20 > EMA50 > EMA200 (strong uptrend). During the 2025 drawdown, this condition was rarely met, limiting signals to just 2-3 trades in that period.

**ofi** generates the most signals (34) but lowest win rate (35.3%) — it's a momentum strategy that works well for trending stocks but MKS.L is mean-reverting.

**Common thread:** All top 3 strategies use **BB Lower + RSI < 35** as the entry signal — confirming that MKS.L's optimal strategy is buying deep oversold dips.

## Phase 4: Custom Strategy — `mks_retail_rebound`

### Design Rationale

The custom strategy combines the best elements from top performers and adds improvements:

**Entry conditions (ALL must be true):**
1. **Close ≤ BB Lower** — price is oversold relative to 20-day range
2. **RSI(14) < 35** — momentum is extremely weak (buying opportunity)
3. **CCI(20) < -100** — commodity channel confirms extreme
4. **CMF > -0.2** — not extreme capitulation (avoids buying stocks in freefall)
5. **GARCH vol not rising** — volatility is stabilizing, not a crash

**Exit conditions (ANY triggers):**
1. **Close ≥ BB Middle** — mean reversion complete, take profit at fair value
2. **Close < 5-day high - 3×ATR** — trailing stop protects gains
3. **RSI(14) > 65** — momentum exhaustion, captures extended bounces

### Improvements over `dual_exit_meanrev`:
- **GARCH filter**: Avoids entries when volatility is spiking (falling knife protection)
- **CMF filter**: Avoids extreme capitulation selloffs with no volume support
- **RSI exit at 65**: Captures extended bounces beyond BB Middle (many bounces go well above the 20-day SMA)

### Parameter Insights

- **CMF threshold**: -0.15 gives best risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe 1.92, DD 5.04%) but fewer trades (3). -0.2 gives best absolute return (26.48%) with 4 trades. Both are superior to the baseline.
- **ATR multiplier**: 2× and 3× are nearly identical. Wider trails (4×, 5×) add trades without improving returns.
- **RSI exit**: 60, 65, and 70 are equivalent (the trailing stop and BB Middle exit trigger before RSI 65 in this period).
- **High window**: 5-day and 3-day produce similar results.

**Decision**: The current strategy uses **CMF > -0.2** (not -0.15) because +26.48% absolute return beats +18.42%, despite Sharpe 1.58 vs 1.92. The 8.49% drawdown is still well below the best built-in (15.99%).

### Why It Works for MKS.L

1. **Fits the V-shaped pattern**: MKS.L drops sharply and rebounds — the strategy buys at the bottom (BB Lower + RSI < 35 + CCI < -100) and rides the bounce.
2. **GARCH filter protects against crashes**: Avoids entries when volatility is spiking, preventing buys during continued selloffs.
3. **CMF filter avoids capitulation**: Requires some volume support (CMF > -0.2), ensuring smart money isn't completely absent.
4. **Three-way exit captures bounces**: BB Middle exit takes quick profits; ATR trail locks in gains on extended moves; RSI exit at 65 catches momentum bounces.

### Key Risks and Limitations

1. **Limited data**: Only 463 trading days (1.8 years) and only 4 trades. The strategy hasn't been tested through a full bear market (only 1.5% of data was bearish).
2. **Overfitting risk**: The CMF > -0.2 threshold was selected from optimization — results may not generalize to out-of-sample data.
3. **Low trade count**: 4 trades over 1.8 years is statistically thin. The 100% win rate may not persist.
4. **No P/E data**: Fundamental filters (e.g., undervaluation) were not incorporated — this is a purely technical strategy.
5. **Sector risk**: UK retail faces structural headwinds (e-commerce shift, cost-of-living pressures). Technical patterns may break down in regime changes.

### Recommendations for Further Testing

- **Walk-forward analysis**: Test on out-of-sample data (e.g., 2022-2024) to confirm the strategy wasn't overfitted to 2025-2026.
- **Add fundamental filters**: Incorporate P/E ratio or revenue growth to avoid buying fundamentally deteriorating retailers.
- **Test across more tickers**: Validate the CMF + GARCH + BB Lower approach on other UK retail stocks (SBRY.L, TJH.L, DPLM.L).
- **Monte Carlo simulation**: Randomize trade order to estimate drawdown and return distribution under different scenarios.
