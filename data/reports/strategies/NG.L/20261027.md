# Investment Strategy Report: National Grid (NG.L)

## Executive Summary

- **Company:** National Grid plc (NG.L)
- **Sector:** Utilities (regulated electricity and gas transmission)
- **Analysis Period:** 10 years (2016-10-17 to 2026-10-27)
- **Best Overall:** bb_meanrev — 29.86% return, 0.42 Sharpe, 15.69% drawdown
- **Highest Return:** bb_meanrev — 29.86% return
- **Lowest Drawdown:** bos_breakout — 0.20% max drawdown (1 trade only)
- **Recommended:** bb_meanrev (BB Mean Reversion)

National Grid is a regulated UK utility with stable cash flows, limited growth upside,
and mean-reverting price action. Trend-following strategies consistently underperform
because NG.L lacks sustained directional trends — it oscillates around its 20-day moving
average with shallow drawdowns that recover naturally. Mean-reversion at Bollinger Band
extremes captures the bulk of available returns.

## Phase 3: Top 5 Strategy Deep Dive

### 1. bb_meanrev — BB Mean Reversion (Recommended)

- **Logic:** Enter when price touches the lower Bollinger Band AND RSI is oversold
  AND Williams %R is extreme. Exit when price reverts to the middle Bollinger Band.
- **Entry conditions:**
  - Close ≤ Bollinger Lower Band (20, 2)
  - RSI(14) < 30
  - Williams %R(14) < −80
- **Exit conditions:**
  - Close ≥ Bollinger Middle Band (20-day SMA)
- **Results:** 29.86% return, 0.42 Sharpe, 15.69% max DD, 2.408 profit factor,
  70.6% win rate (12 wins / 5 losses), 17 trades over 10 years
- **Average win:** +4.04%, Average loss: −4.04% (symmetric — classic mean-reversion)
- **Largest win:** +12.26%, Largest loss: −7.18%
- **Analysis:** This is the canonical mean-reversion setup. NG.L's tight range-bound
  behavior means price reliably reverts to the 20-day SMA after touching the lower
  band. The three-condition entry (BB Lower + RSI + W%R) filters out weak signals,
  yielding a 70.6% win rate. With only 17 trades over 10 years (~1.7 per year), this
  is a low-frequency strategy — each trade represents a significant dip.

### 2. bb_meanrev_optimized — BB MeanRev Opt. (Config 1)

- **Logic:** Identical signals to bb_meanrev (same entry/exit). The "optimized"
  label refers to documented exit enhancements (5% profit target, 120-day forced
  exit) that were analyzed in prior custom simulations but are not enforced in the
  vectorbt signal implementation.
- **Results:** 29.86% return, 0.42 Sharpe, 15.69% max DD — identical to baseline.
- **Analysis:** In the current vectorbt implementation, this is a duplicate of
  bb_meanrev. The optimization insights (time-stop 120d, profit target 5%) would
  require custom post-processing simulation to realize the full potential.

### 3. bb_meanrev_upper — BB MeanRev (Upper Exit)

- **Logic:** Same entry as baseline, but exit at the UPPER band instead of middle.
  This aims to capture the full range of mean-reversion.
- **Entry conditions:** Same as bb_meanrev (BB Lower + RSI < 30 + W%R < −80)
- **Exit conditions:** Close ≥ Bollinger Upper Band (instead of middle band)
- **Results:** 20.20% return, 0.26 Sharpe, 22.45% max DD, 1.940 profit factor,
  69.2% win rate, 13 trades
- **Average win:** +4.79%, Average loss: −5.35%
- **Analysis:** Targeting the upper band captures more upside per trade (+4.79% vs
  +4.04%), but also allows losses to grow wider (−5.35% vs −4.04%). The higher
  max drawdown (22.45% vs 15.69%) shows that waiting for the upper band exposes
  the portfolio to larger temporary losses. Sharpe is lower because the risk-
  adjusted return doesn't justify the extra drawdown.

### 4. bb_meanrev_entry2 — BB MeanRev Entry2 (Config 2)

- **Logic:** Adds quality filters to the baseline entry — requires wide Bollinger
  Bands and sufficient ATR to confirm the setup has enough room for reversion.
- **Entry conditions:**
  - Close ≤ Bollinger Lower Band
  - RSI(14) < 30
  - Williams %R(14) < −80
  - BB Width ≥ 10% (bandwidth expansion = room to revert)
  - ATR(14) ≥ 15 (sufficient volatility)
- **Exit conditions:** Close ≥ Bollinger Middle Band
- **Results:** 18.70% return, 0.31 Sharpe, 16.69% max DD, 2.280 profit factor,
  66.7% win rate, 9 trades (half the frequency of baseline)
- **Average win:** +5.25%, Average loss: −4.26% (better asymmetric profile)
- **Analysis:** The Entry2 filter significantly reduces signal frequency (9 vs 17
  trades) but each trade is higher quality. The win rate is slightly lower (66.7%
  vs 70.6%), but the profit factor is higher (2.28 vs 2.41) and wins are bigger
  (+5.25% vs +4.04%). The filter works for NG.L because wide band expansions
  indicate meaningful dips that tend to revert fully.

### 5. bb_meanrev_atr — BB MeanRev (ATR Trail)

- **Logic:** Relaxes the entry criteria (RSI < 35 instead of < 30, no Williams %R
  filter) but adds an ATR trailing stop as a secondary exit condition.
- **Entry conditions:**
  - Close ≤ Bollinger Lower Band
  - RSI(14) < 35
- **Exit conditions:**
  - Close ≥ Bollinger Middle Band, OR
  - Close < Highest Close(5) − 4 × ATR(14) (trailing stop)
- **Results:** 12.34% return, 0.19 Sharpe, 25.81% max DD, 1.240 profit factor,
  71.9% win rate, 32 trades
- **Average win:** +3.12%, Average loss: −6.15% (negative asymmetry)
- **Analysis:** The ATR trailing stop is counterproductive for NG.L. Mean-reversion
  trades on utility stocks benefit from letting winners run to the middle band.
  The trailing stop cuts winners short (+3.12% vs +4.04%) and doesn't adequately
  protect against losses (avg loss is −6.15%). The higher trade count (32 vs 17)
  with lower per-trade returns indicates the strategy is overtrading.

## Phase 4: Custom Strategy Assessment

No custom strategy was needed. All top 5 performers are built-in BB mean-reversion
variants. The dominant pattern is clear: NG.L's price action is fundamentally
mean-reverting, and the bb_meanrev entry conditions (BB Lower + RSI < 30 + W%R < −80)
are well-calibrated for this asset.

However, the existing vectorbt implementation has a limitation: it only supports
simple entry/exit signals. Better exits identified through custom analysis include:

- **Time-stop 120 days:** Prior analysis showed a 120-day forced exit yields
  96.1% total return with 0% max drawdown and Sharpe 12.35 (in custom simulation
  on the same entry signals). This would significantly outperform the BB Middle
  exit (29.86% return, 15.69% max DD).
- **Dual exit (BB Middle OR RSI > 50):** Captures quick profits at either
  condition — Sharpe 7.74, 48.9% return in prior custom analysis.

These improvements would require implementing time-stop logic outside of vectorbt's
signal-based API (e.g., post-processing trades by holding period).

## Phase 6: Final Recommendation

### Best Overall: bb_meanrev (BB Mean Reversion)

**Rationale:** Highest risk-adjusted return among strategies implementable with
vectorbt's standard signal API. 29.86% total return over 10 years vs. 5.49% for
buy-and-hold — a 5.4x multiple. 70.6% win rate with symmetric win/loss sizes
(+4.04% / −4.04%) and manageable 15.69% max drawdown.

### Entry Rules (exact)

```text
1. Close ≤ ta_bband_lower (Bollinger Lower Band, 20, 2)
2. AND ta_rsi14 < 30
3. AND ta_williams_r < −80
```

All three conditions must be met simultaneously. No direction filter needed —
mean-reversion works in both bull and bear markets.

### Exit Rules (exact)

```text
Primary (default, vectorbt-compatible):
  Close ≥ ta_bband_middle (20-day SMA)

Recommended enhancements (custom simulation):
  Dual exit: Close ≥ ta_bband_middle OR ta_rsi14 > 50
  Time-stop: Forced exit 120 days from entry
```

### Risk Management

| Parameter | Value | Rationale |
|-----------|-------|-----------|
| Position sizing | 2% portfolio risk | Conservative for low-frequency utility strategy |
| Stop loss | None (implicit in BB Middle exit) | Mean-reversion exit serves as natural stop |
| Max positions | 1 | Only trade one mean-reversion setup at a time |
| Max holding period | 120 days (recommended) | Prevents capital lock-up in extended drawdowns |
| Re-entry cooldown | None | New signals can appear immediately |

### When This Strategy Fails

1. **Sustained downtrend:** If NG.L enters a multi-year bear market (like 2008 or
   2018), BB Lower breaches become frequent and the middle band may never be
   reached before the next breach. The 2008 crash saw NG.L drop 17% — a mean-reversion
   entry would have been stopped out with a loss, and the next opportunity wouldn't
   appear for months.

2. **Low-volatility compression:** When BB Width is extremely narrow (< 1%), the
   mean-reversion range is too small to generate meaningful returns. The Entry2
   filter (BB Width ≥ 10%, ATR ≥ 15) helps avoid these periods.

3. **Earnings/gap events:** As a utility stock, NG.L can gap down on regulatory
   changes, dividend announcements, or macro events. These gaps may skip past the
   BB Lower entirely, leaving no entry signal, or breach the BB Lower and continue
   falling (a "falling knife" scenario).

4. **Extended bull runs:** If NG.L enters a sustained uptrend (like 2019's +22%),
   the BB Lower may be rarely touched, resulting in few or zero signals. The
   strategy is opportunistic — it profits from dips, not from trending markets.

### Suggested Improvements

1. **Implement time-stop exits** — Use post-processing to apply 120-day forced exits,
   which prior custom analysis showed yields ~96% return with 0% max DD.

2. **Add BB Width filter** — Adopt the Entry2 filter (BB Width ≥ 10%, ATR ≥ 15) for
   higher-quality signals with fewer trades.

3. **Regime filter** — Only take mean-reversion entries when HMM regime is NOT
   strongly bearish (ta_hmm_regime_prob_1 > ta_hmm_regime_prob_0). This avoids
   catching falling knives during confirmed bear markets.

4. **Volatility filter** — Skip entries when ta_garch_vol is rising sharply, as this
   indicates increasing uncertainty and potential for larger-than-usual drawdowns.
