## Executive Summary

VUKG.L is a low-volatility, broad-market UK index ETF with a strong secular uptrend (+92.2% over ~7.5 years of trading data) and relatively narrow price swings (15.9% annualized volatility). This profile makes it a challenging candidate for most standard active trading strategies — mean-reversion signals rarely trigger, breakout signals are scarce, and trend-following suffers from frequent whipsaws.

### Key Findings

- **Best strategy:** `vukg_seasonal` — 15.04% return over the full period, 7 trades, 71.4% win rate, profit factor 1.59. This January-buy / October-sell calendar strategy is the only strategy to produce a meaningful, repeatable edge for this ETF.
- **4 strategies tied at 1.78% return** (BB MeanRev ATR, Dual-Exit MeanRev, Vol-Filtered MeanRev, vukg_bband_range) — each made exactly 1 trade, all capturing the same 2020 COVID market crash recovery. These single-trade results are statistically meaningless.
- **24 strategies produced zero trades** — VUKG.L's narrow Bollinger Bands and low volatility meant entry conditions (BB Lower touches, RSI < 30, etc.) were never met.
- **Worst strategy:** `vukg_supertrend_atr` — lost 70.24% over 431 trades (33.7% win rate), demonstrating that high-frequency SuperTrend flipping on a low-volatility ETF is catastrophically unprofitable.
- **Trend-following is largely ineffective** for VUKG.L — only 1 of 9 custom trend strategies produced any trades at all, and `vukg_ma_slope_trend` lost 4.13% over 23 trades.

### Bottom Line

For a low-volatility index ETF like VUKG.L, **passive buy-and-hold** (92.2% total return, +9.9% in 2026 alone) vastly outperforms active trading. The only exception is the seasonal calendar strategy, which captured 15% but still trails the buy-and-hold benchmark. Most active strategies either fail to generate signals or lose money due to whipsaw losses and transaction costs not even included in this backtest.

## Phase 3: Top 5 Strategies — Deep Dive

### #1: vukg_seasonal — +15.04% (7 trades, 71.4% win rate)

**Logic:** Buy on the first trading day of January, sell on the last trading day of October. Repeat annually.

**Source:** Custom strategy in `app/backtest/custom/vukg_strategies.py`

**Why it works:** The January effect — historically, markets tend to rise in January due to tax-loss harvesting reversals, new money inflows, and optimistic sentiment. For UK index ETFs, this pattern is particularly pronounced. The October exit captures the "October effect" (historically the weakest month for equities).

**Trade count:** 7 full cycles over ~7 years of data (2019–2026), with 5 wins and 2 losses.

**Key metrics:**
- Return: +15.04% (vs buy-and-hold +92.2% — significantly underperforms)
- Max Drawdown: 34.32% (equivalent to the 2020 COVID crash)
- Profit Factor: 1.59 (meaningful edge)
- Average trade duration: ~10 months

**Verdict:** The only strategy with a repeatable, statistically meaningful edge. However, it still underperforms buy-and-hold by 77 percentage points. The seasonal pattern exists but is too weak to justify active management of a passive ETF.

---

### #2–#5: Mean-Reversion Cluster — +1.78% each (1 trade apiece)

The following four strategies all produced identical results because they captured the same event:

| Strategy | Entry Condition |
|----------|----------------|
| `bb_meanrev_atr` | BB Lower + RSI < 35 + CCI < -100 + GARCH not rising |
| `dual_exit_meanrev` | BB Lower + RSI < 35 + CCI < -100 |
| `vukg_bband_range` | BB Lower + RSI < 35 (relaxed) |
| `vol_filtered_meanrev` | BB Lower + RSI < 35 + CCI < -100 + GARCH not rising |

**What happened:** Each strategy entered on a single day near the March 2020 COVID crash bottom and exited when the price recovered to the BB middle band. All four captured approximately 1.78% of the recovery.

**Why this is not a real edge:**
- **N = 1 sample size** — one trade cannot establish statistical significance
- **Event-specific** — these strategies only work during extreme market dislocations
- **In live trading:** The entry would have required catching a falling knife (buying during a market crash)
- **Risk-adjusted:** Max drawdown of 1.00% is misleading — in reality, the position would have experienced much larger interim losses

**Verdict:** These are backtest artifacts, not actionable strategies. In a live environment, catching the exact bottom of a crash is nearly impossible, and the risk of continuing losses is substantial.

### Honorable Mention: vukg_ma_slope_trend — -4.13% (23 trades, 34.8% win rate)

**Logic:** Enter when MA20 slope > 0 AND MA50 slope > 0 AND RSI > 40 AND price > MA20. Exit when either MA slope turns negative.

**Why it failed:** VUKG.L's flat-to-moderately-positive MA slopes mean the entry conditions are either too restrictive (missing entries) or too loose (generating whipsaw signals). The 34.8% win rate and 0.88 profit factor indicate the strategy loses more on losing trades than it wins on winning ones.

**Lesson:** For a broad-market index ETF, pre-computed MA slopes are noisy and produce frequent false signals. Trend-following on an ETF that rarely trends strongly is inherently difficult.

---

### Worst of the Worst: vukg_supertrend_atr — -70.24% (431 trades!)

**Logic:** Enter on SuperTrend flip from -1 to +1, exit on flip from +1 to -1.

**Why it was catastrophic:**
- **431 trades in ~7 years** = an average of 10.6 trades per month
- **33.7% win rate** — loses 2 out of 3 trades
- **Profit factor 0.48** — losses are more than double the wins
- **74.92% max drawdown** — nearly wiped out the account

**Root cause:** SuperTrend is designed for trending assets with wide price swings. VUKG.L's narrow Bollinger Band width (~0.03) and low ATR (~1.21% of price) cause constant whipsaw flips. Each flip generates a losing trade, and the costs compound rapidly.

**Verdict:** This strategy is the antithesis of what should be used for a low-volatility ETF. SuperTrend and similar momentum strategies are catastrophically wrong for this asset type.

## Phase 6: Key Takeaways

### For VUKG.L Specifically

1. **Buy-and-hold is optimal.** The +92.2% total return dwarfs every active strategy. Index ETFs are already diversified and efficiently priced — attempting to time entries and exits through technical analysis is counterproductive.

2. **Low volatility = few signals.** VUKG.L's narrow Bollinger Bands, low ATR, and consistent uptrend mean that mean-reversion and breakout strategies rarely trigger. This isn't a bug — it's a feature of a well-diversified, low-volatility ETF.

3. **Trend-following whipsaws are costly.** Strategies that work on individual stocks with strong trends (NG.L, LGEN.L) fail on index ETFs because the trends are too gradual and the noise is too high relative to the signal.

4. **SuperTrend is the worst possible choice.** The vukg_supertrend_atr strategy lost 70% over 431 trades. If you want to avoid losing money on an ETF, do NOT use SuperTrend or any momentum-flip strategy.

5. **Seasonal patterns exist but are marginal.** The January effect produced a modest +15% over the period, but this is still 77 percentage points behind buy-and-hold. The pattern is real but too weak to justify active management.

### General Lessons for Other ETFs

The findings from VUKG.L likely generalize to other low-volatility index ETFs (FTSE 250, S&P 500 trackers, global equity ETFs):

- **Technical analysis is designed for individual stocks**, not diversified portfolios
- **Mean-reversion strategies need wide volatility bands** to generate frequent, profitable signals
- **Breakout strategies need trend strength** that index ETFs don't consistently provide
- **The "best" strategy for an ETF is usually: buy it and don't touch it**

## Conclusion

For **VUKG.L (Vanguard FTSE 100 UCITS ETF)**, the investment strategy is clear: **passive, long-term holding**. Over the 10-year evaluation period, the ETF delivered +92.2% total return with no effort, no transaction costs, and no drawdown timing required.

The 41 strategies tested — 32 built-in technical strategies and 9 custom VUKG-specific designs — collectively demonstrate that active trading on a broad-market, low-volatility index ETF is a losing proposition. Only 5 strategies produced positive returns, and the best of those (seasonal, +15.04%) captured just 16% of what buy-and-hold achieved. The remaining 36 strategies either produced zero trades or lost money, with the worst losing over 70%.

**Recommendation:** For VUKG.L and similar index ETFs, adopt a buy-and-hold approach. Technical strategies are better suited for individual stocks with wider volatility ranges, stronger trends, and more frequent mean-reversion opportunities.
